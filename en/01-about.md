The 15th UCPC, <sup>UCPC 2026</sup> will be held!

## Overview

UCPC is a annual summertime competition hosted by Union of Clubs for Programming Contests. Starting from the 1st UCPC hosted in 2011, there have been a total of 14 UCPCs as of the year 2025. The 15th UCPC, scheduled for the summer of 2026 is currently in preparation.

UCPC both serves as a place of trial for teams preparing for ICPC, and as a forum of discussion for university students who are interested in competitive programming. Students from across South Korea engage in fierce competition and grow from the experiences they get from UCPC.

## Eligibility

- Anyone who meets the following criteria is eligible for UCPC
  - Undergraduates and master's program students.(students who are currently enrolled, on leave of absence, or completed their coursework are all allowed, except for graduates)
  - Finalists must be able to submit supporting documents to verify their status.
  - Supporting documents must be issued within 90 days of the final contest date, and documents must be of a type that allows Unions of Clubs for Programming Contests to verify the participant's current academic status.(ex: Certificate of Enrollment, Certificate of Student Record, Certificate of Leave of Absence)
- Teams may consist of students from different universities.
- Students who are not from a club affiliated with UCPC are also admitted.



## Contest Information

### Topics

- The contest features problems centering on Computer Science and Mathematics. Possible topics include data structures, graph theory, dynamic programming, mathematics and geometry.
- The preliminary round consists of approximately 10 problems and the final round consists of approximately 12 problems. The problems generally follow the ICPC seoul reginal trends.
- The problems are provided either in English or in Korean.
- Scores are evaluated based on the number of problems a team solved accurately within a given time limit.

### Format

- Participants solve problems by writing source code that generates the correct output and submitting the written code to the Online Judge system.
- The system executes the submitted source code and returns either "accepted" or "wrong answer" as the verdict.
- Once the contest officially begins, participants must access the pre-announced contest platform to view and solve the problems.
- (Preliminary Round) Team members are allowed to work on their individual computers.
- (Final Round) Each team is limited to one computer per team.

### Ranking

- From the submission history, each team receives a penalty score aside from the number of solved problems.
- A team's penalty is the sum of the penalties for each problem calculated by the following formula. 
  - (Submission time of the first submission that is accepted - starting time of the contest)+(number of     wrong submissions before the first accepted submission) × 20
- Teams are rated based on the following criteria, applied in order
  - Highest number of problems solved
  - Lowest total penalty accumulated
  - Earliest time of the last correct submission
